## The shade confusion starts with the 2024 reformulation

The old NARS powder blush was a pressed talc formula with a cult following built almost entirely on one shade: Orgasm. Peachy pink with golden shimmer. It has been the brand's bestseller for years and the standard recommendation for "I want one blush and I don't want to think about it."

NARS has since moved to a talc-free formula and renumbered things. On the current product pages you'll see **Orgasm listed as 777** and **Orgasm Edge as 778**. Orgasm Edge is the matte version of the same peachy pink family. The new powder is listed with a **16-hour wear claim** and a "pure pigment blend" of four pigment types; NARS also notes that the strongest color payoff hits at the first point of brush contact, which is why heavy-handed application on the new formula tends to look blotchier than the old one.

That numbering change is why you'll find reviews on the brand's own product pages from people asking which new shade matches the old Sin, and replies pointing them to Orgasm or Deep Throat depending on whether the old one pulled too peachy. It's also why resale listings get confusing — older packaging with the old shade codes is still floating around.

## Which format you pick matters more than the shade

There are three things in this line that people lump together as "NARS blush," and they behave very differently:

**Powder blush (talc-free, 4.8 g / 0.16 oz).** Matte, satin and shimmer finishes. This is the one that lasts all day, layers over foundation without dragging it, and needs a light hand. Best if you want one product and you want it to stay put through a workday.

**Afterglow Liquid Blush (7 ml).** A cream-to-liquid formula with a doe-foot applicator, sodium hyaluronate, vegan protein, and a stated 8 hours of hydration. It's buildable, transfer-resistant, and much more forgiving on dry or textured skin. Powder sits on top of it well if you want more intensity.

**Sets and duos.** NARS periodically bundles full-size products — for example the Orgasm Matte & Glow Blush Duo pairs Orgasm 777 with Orgasm Edge 778, and the Orgasm Afterglow Liquid Blush & Lip Balm Duo pairs the liquid blush with a matching balm.

If you have dry skin or you're new to blush, liquid first. If you have oily skin or you want the "sixteen hours and done" version, powder. If you already know you love Orgasm specifically, a duo is usually the better value per gram than two singles.

## The same compact for $28 or €41 — why

Three things drive the spread:

**Regional pricing.** NARS sets prices per market. €41.00 in France, Germany, Italy and Spain is not a markup on a $36 US price in any meaningful sense — it's a different price list, and EU prices include VAT while most US listing prices don't include sales tax.

**Promotional cycles.** The German site showing €28.70 against a €41.00 list is a straight discount from the brand itself. Those windows are short and usually tied to a season or a specific retailer event.

**Channel.** A department store, the brand's own site, a beauty specialty retailer and a discounter all price the same compact differently, and the discounter's copy is usually older stock or grey-market stock.

That spread is exactly the gap that cashback is designed to close a little. It won't turn a $36 compact into a $20 one. It can shift a purchase enough that the difference between buying today and waiting for a sale stops mattering much.

## When to skip the cashback route entirely

- **You're buying a single low-priced item during a full-price period.** The rebate won't cover the mental overhead.
- **You want to use a promo code you found somewhere else.** Many merchants exclude cashback on orders made with non-platform codes. Check terms before assuming you can stack both.
- **You're buying from a marketplace seller rather than the brand or a major retailer.** Third-party sellers are frequently not eligible merchants, and cashback won't track even if you clicked through correctly.
- **You need the product in hand today.** Cashback doesn't change your checkout total.

## Buying a real NARS blush, not a convincing one

The discount listings are tempting and the risk is straightforward. Orgasm is one of the most counterfeited blushes in the world, precisely because it's recognisable and expensive. A few practical filters:

- NARS blush is **4.8 g / 0.16 oz**. A listing claiming full size at a suspiciously low price and a different weight is a red flag.
- Buy from the brand, a major beauty retailer, or a department store when the price gap is small. If a seller is 50% below list on a sealed current-shade compact, ask why.
- Sealed current-formula stock should have intact packaging and a plastic protector over the powder. Product pages for the new blush carry reviews from buyers who received compacts with fingerprints and no protective film — worth reading before ordering from a warehouse-based seller.

## Questions that come up most

**Does cashback apply to sale-priced NARS items?** Usually yes, calculated on what you actually pay, unless the merchant excludes sale items in its terms. Check the merchant page.

**Can I stack a cashback portal with a NARS site-wide discount?** Often yes, because the discount is applied by the brand rather than through a code you entered from elsewhere. Codes from third-party coupon sites are the common failure point.

**Is the liquid or powder blush better value?** Powder is 4.8 g; liquid is 7 ml of a formula that spreads further per use. Liquid usually runs out faster in practice, so the powder tends to last longer per dollar despite similar list prices.

**Why is Orgasm Edge more expensive to find?** It's the matte counterpart to a shimmer shade with enormous demand. It sells out, and sold-out shades get marked up by non-official sellers.

**Which shade for medium-deep skin?** Orgasm works as a warm flush on many tones; if the golden shimmer pulls too light, Deep Throat and Dolce Vita read pinker, and Taj Mahal and Gina are the deeper warm options in the powder line.

**Do I need both a liquid and a powder?** No. Liquid over powder is a layering technique for intensity, not a requirement. Pick one format, then add the second only if you specifically want a longer-wearing finish over a cream base.
